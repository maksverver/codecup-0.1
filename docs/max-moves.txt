Maximum number of moves in a valid game state
---------------------------------------------

tl;dr: the maximum number of moves is 256

To calculate an initial upper bound on the maximum number of moves, consider
that I can control at most 1 wazir, 2 knights, 4 ferzes, 8 dabbabbas, 16 alfils,
which all can move in up to 4 directions, except for the knight which can do 8,
for an upper bound of (1 + 4 + 8 + 16)*4 + 2*8 = 132 moves.

In addition to that, I may have pieces of each of the 4 types in hand, and up to
62 empty fields to drop them on, for a maximum number of 4*62 == 248 drop moves.

This gives an initial upper bound of 132 + 248 = 380 moves, though clearly the
real maximum must be less, because some of these cases overlap (I cannot have
all pieces on the board _and_ some in hand, for example).

But we can do better! First, observe that it is indeed optimal for me to control
all the pieces (except the opponent's wazir, which I cannot capture without
ending the game), since moving an opponent's piece to my hand (whether from his
hand or from the board) cannot invalidate any move I had previously, but it can
create additional drop moves.

Secondly, note that since the number of pieces on the board is at most 32, each
piece type can be dropped in at least 64 - 32 = 32 empty spaces. That means it's
optimal to have at least one piece of each type on hand, since a piece on board
can give me at most 8 moves (the knight).

Now we can assume that I have a piece of each type on hand. But that means
moving a piece other than a knight from the board to my hand will not reduce the
total number of moves, because I lose at most 4 moves on the board, but gain
exactly 4 drop moves: 1 for each piece type, onto the vacated square.

Following this logic, I should move all pieces to my hand, except for my second
knight. Then, it's easy to create a situation where my Wazir and Knight each
have the maximal number of moves:

    . o . . . . . .
    o W o . . . . .
    . o . . o . o .
    . . . o . . . o
    . . . . . N . .
    . . . o . . . o
    . w . . o . o .
    . . . . . . . .

There are 4 Wazir moves, 8 Knight moves, and 4 * 61 drop moves, for a total of
4 + 8 + 4*61 = 256 moves.
