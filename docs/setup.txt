Each player has 16 total pieces of 5 types:

  1. Wazir    0.1   1x
  2. Knight   1.2   1x
  3. Ferz     1.1   2x
  4. Dabbaba  0.2   4x
  5. Alfil    2.2   8x

Only the Wazir and Knight can reach every square on the board.

The other piece types have the property that they are locked to disjoint subsets
of the boards (like bishops in chess, which can cover either the black or the
white squares, depending on where they start), but the number of copies of each
piece matches the number of subsets, so by placing one piece on each subset we
can effectively reach the entire board with each piece type (and practically, it
seems beneficial to do so). It also has the benefit that those pieces of the
same type cannot block each other, though the downside is that such pieces
cannot protect each other either.

So for the starting configuration, I want to create a setup where the pieces of
each type are distributed over different subsets.

For Alfil (2.2):

    1 2 3 4 1 2 3 4
    5 6 7 8 5 6 7 8

(This means one Alfil must be placed on one of the two fields labeled 1,
one on a field labeled 2, one on a field labeled 3, etc.)

A total of 2**8 = 256 arrangements.

For Dabbaba (0.2):

    1 2 1 2 1 2 1 2
    3 4 3 4 3 4 3 4

A total of 4**4 = 256 arrangements.

For Ferz (1.1):

    1 2 1 2 1 2 1 2
    2 1 2 1 2 1 2 1

A total of 8**2 = 64 arrangements.

However, some of these arrangements overlap.

In particular, placing the Alfils removes exactly 2 out of the 4 dabbaba
placements for each of the 4 sets. And placing Alfils and Dabbabas removes
exactly 6 out of 8 Ferz placements for each of 2 sets.

That means we effectively have 2**8 = 256 choices for placing Alfils, then
2**4 = 16 choices for placing Dabbabas, then 2**2 choices for placing Ferzes,
then finaly we have 2 choices for placing the Knight and Wazir, for a total
of 2**(8 + 4 + 2 + 1) = 2**15 = 32768 possible arrangements.

See tools/enum-setup.py for code to generate the possible arrangements.


POSITION OF THE WAZIR
---------------------

It seems beneficial to start with the Wazir on the back row and near the corner.
I determined this by running a tournament of 10,000 games with my player for
test competition 2 playing aginst itself, then using the eval-setup.py to
calculate the winrate for each starting position of the Wazir.

% tools/arbiter.py --rounds=5000 --logdir=logs/ -t 4 player/backups/submission-4-id-27770/release/play player/backups/submission-4-id-27770/release/play

0.559 0.579 0.476 0.424 0.442 0.469 0.572 0.560

This shows that the best positions of the Wazir are a2/a7 and h2/h7 (back row,
next to the corner), with the corners a1/a8 and h1/h8 being close seconds.

It makes intuitive sense: if the Wazir is in the corner, it can be attacked from
fewer spaces. And having it not exactly in the corner gives it an additional
space to move to, if the corner piece is moved out of the way first.


POSITION OF THE FERZES
----------------------

I haven't proven it but I suspect Ferzes should be placed on the front row,
since they are the only pieces that cannot jump over another piece, so when
placed on the back row they would be blocked at the start. Additionally, they
move the slowest so to maximize their influence they should probably start
closer to the center.
